# Corrections and conventions

Places where a Lean statement differs from Miao's Chapter 2 (draft of 21 April 2013): a claim
corrected, a hypothesis made explicit, or a convention chosen. Corrections to the Chapter 1
results carried over here are in the [Chapter 1 corrections](../../DifferenceEquations/docs/corrections.md).

## Corrections

| Where | Book | Lean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p. 47 | If all eigenvalues of `Φ` are inside the unit circle, `{z_t}` has a unique stationary distribution. | Not under (2.1)–(2.2), which restrict only first and second conditional moments: with `Φ = 0`, `Σ = 1`, shocks uniform on `{−√2, 0, 0, √2}` and on `{−1, 1, 1, −1}` both satisfy (2.2) and give covariance-stationary solutions with the same moments but `Ez_t⁴ = 2` and `1` (`stationary_law_not_unique`). What is unique is the stationary mean and autocovariances (`var_stationary_moments`, `var_stationary_of_initial`, `lyap_unique`). |
| Definition 2.1.1, p. 47 | The covariance `E(X_t − m)(X_s − m)′` depends only on `|t − s|`. | For vector processes it is `Γ(t − s)` for `t ≥ s` and `Γ(s − t)′` for `t < s`, and `Γ` need not be symmetric (`definition_2_1_1_not_symmetric`, `Φ = [[0, 1/2], [0, 0]]`, `Σ = I`). The definition is stated with the lag `j ≥ 0` (`var_IsCovStationary`). |
| (2.10), p. 51 | Take `E_{t−1}`; the result is "similar to (2.7) and can be solved in the same way"; then recover `x_t`. | The reduced equation contains `x_{t−1}`, not `E_{t−1}x_{t−1}` (`eq_2_10_reduce`). The recovery needs `a₀ ≠ λ₁`, not `a₀ ≠ 0`: with `E_tx_{t+1} = λ₁x_t + ω_t` substituted, the coefficient of `x_t` is `a₀ − λ₁`. Under that condition there is a unique stable solution (`eq_2_10_solution`, `eq_2_10_unique`); for `a₀ = λ₁` there are sunspot solutions (`eq_2_10_indeterminate`). |
| (2.17), p. 56 | `u_t = −T₂₂⁻¹ Σⱼ (T₂₂⁻¹S₂₂)ʲ Q₂CE_tz_{t+j}`. | The misprint of (1.37) repeated: `u_t = −Σⱼ (T₂₂⁻¹S₂₂)ʲ T₂₂⁻¹Q₂CE_tz_{t+j}` (`eq_2_17`, `eq_2_17_solution`); the printed form fails already for `E_t = id` (`eq_2_17_printed_false`). The remaining formulas on pp. 56–57 are right (`eq_klein_Es`, `eq_klein_xi`, `eq_klein_s`, `eq_klein_k`). |
| p. 57 | In the LQ problem with `{ξ_t}` a white noise, `{x_t}` is predetermined with prediction error `{ξ_t}`. | The prediction error is `C(ξ_{t+1} − E_tξ_{t+1})` (`lq_predError`); it is `Cξ_{t+1}` when `ξ` is a martingale difference (`lq_predetermined`). A white noise need not be one (`whiteNoise_not_mds`). |
| (2.22), p. 59 | `u_t = −T₂₂⁻¹ Σ_{j≥1} (T₂₂⁻¹S₂₂)ʲ Q₂.(Ψε_{t+j} + Πη_{t+j})`. | `u_t = −Σ_{j≥1} (T₂₂⁻¹S₂₂)^{j−1} T₂₂⁻¹Q₂.(Ψε_{t+j} + Πη_{t+j})` (`eq_2_22`); the printed form fails for `S₂₂ = 0`, `T₂₂ = 1` (`eq_2_22_printed_false`). That `u_t = 0` uses both stability (for the forward sum) and the martingale-difference property of `ε` and `η` (`eq_2_22_expect`). |
| Lemma 2.3.1, proof, p. 60 | The `j`-th column of `Λ` is an `η_t` solving (2.23) for `ε_t = I_j`. | It is minus that `η_t` (`lemma_2_3_1`). |
| p. 61 | `Θ₁ = Z [[S₁₁, S₁₂ − ΞS₂₂], [0, I]]⁻¹ [[T₁₁, T₁₂ − ΞT₂₂], [0, 0]]`. | A trailing `Zᴴ` is missing: `y_t = Θ₁y_{t−1} + Θ₂ε_t` needs `Θ₁ = ZG⁻¹HZᴴ` (`simsTheta1`); the printed `Θ₁` fails for `Γ₀ = 1`, `Γ₁ = 1/2`, `Q = Z = −1` (`theta_1_printed_false`). `Θ₂`, `Θ₃` and (2.24) are right. |
| Theorem 2.3.1, p. 61 | Assumptions 2.3.1–2.3.2 are necessary and sufficient for a unique stable solution. | Sufficient (`theorem_2_3_1_sufficiency`): existence needs only Assumption 2.3.1 (`theorem_2_3_1_existence`) and uniqueness of `y` only Assumption 2.3.2 (`theorem_2_3_1_uniqueness`). Necessity fails in general: if `E_t = id` every martingale difference is `0`, and a system violating both assumptions has a unique stable solution (`theorem_2_3_1_necessity_false`). It holds when the filtration carries a nonzero stable martingale difference (`SimsRich`, `theorem_2_3_1`), which is satisfiable (`simsRich_trunc`). "Unique" is uniqueness of `y` given `y₀` with `Z₂ᴴy₀ = 0`, not of `(y, η)`. |
| p. 62 | "If `B`'s SVD is `TCW`, `A`'s column space includes `B`'s iff `(I − UUᴴ)T = 0`." | With the full unitary `U` the condition is vacuous (`colspace_printed_false`); the test is `(I − U.1U.1ᴴ)B = 0` (`colspace_iff`, `colspace_iff_thin`). |
| Corollary 2.3.1, p. 62 | Given Assumption 2.3.1, the stable solution is unique iff `m = r`. | False for `y`: with `Γ₀ = 1`, `Γ₁ = 2`, `Π = [1 0]` (a prediction error that enters nowhere) `r = 1 < m = 2`, yet `y` is unique (`corollary_2_3_1_printed_false`). `y` is unique iff Assumption 2.3.2 holds, for a rich filtration (`simsY_unique_iff`); `m = r` is the condition for the pair `(y, η)` (`corollary_2_3_1`), and implies Assumption 2.3.2 (`xi_formula`). |
| p. 62 | Lubik and Schorfheide (2003): if `m > r` there are infinitely many stable solutions. | True for `(y, η)` (`lubik_schorfheide`), false for `y` (`corollary_2_3_1_printed_false`). |
| p. 58 | `C = 0` without loss of generality. | Needs `Γ₀ − Γ₁` invertible (`eq_2_19_const`); otherwise the change of variables may not exist (`eq_2_19_const_singular`). |
| p. 67 | `ȳ = g(x̄, 0)` and `x̄ = h(x̄, 0)`, as consequences of the steady state. | They are assumptions: a fixed point of `h` gives a steady state (`eq_steady_state`), but not conversely: `f₁ = (x′ − x)(x′ − 2x)`, `η = 0`, `h = 2x` has the steady state `x̄ = 1` with `h(1, 0) = 2` (`steady_state_printed_false`). |
| p. 67 | "For a unique solution to exist, we must have `g_σ = h_σ = 0`" (and likewise `g_σx = h_σx = 0`, p. 69). | Zero always solves the homogeneous system, and is the only solution iff its 2×2 determinant is nonzero (`pert_hom2_unique`, `theorem_g_sigma_zero`, `theorem_g_sigma_x_zero`). Otherwise it is not forced even for a genuine solution: with `f = (y − y′, x′ − x)`, `η = 1`, `ε = ±1`, `h = x`, the policy `g = σ` has `g_σ = 1` (`g_sigma_not_forced`) and `g = σx` has `g_σx = 1` (`g_sigma_x_not_forced`). |
| p. 68 | `F_xx`: the first term `f_{y′y}g_xh_x`. | The chain rule gives `f_{y′y′}g_xh_x` (`eq_F_xx`); the printed display differs by `(f_{y′y′} − f_{y′y})(g_xh_x)²` (`eq_F_xx_printed`) and fails for `f = y′²`, `g = h = x` (`eq_F_xx_printed_false`). Every other term matches. |
| p. 69 | `F_σσ`, with factors `I`. | The `I` must be `E[ε′²]`, which the book never normalizes to one (`eq_F_sigma_sigma`, `eq_F_sigma_sigma_printed`); the printed display fails for `f = x′²`, `ε′ = ±2` (`eq_F_sigma_sigma_printed_false`). The display also uses `g_σ = h_σ = 0`. |
| p. 69 | `F_σx = 0` is a homogeneous system for `g_σx`, `h_σx`. | Only given `g_σ = h_σ = 0`; otherwise terms such as `f_{y′}g_xxh_σh_x` appear (`eq_F_sigma_x`). |
| p. 68 | (2.33) is equivalent to (2.31). | (2.31) gives (2.33) (`eq_2_33`); the converse needs `x̂₀ ≠ 0` (`eq_2_31_of_2_33`). |
| Ex 2(a), p. 82 | `δ > 1` is necessary and sufficient for a unique stable equilibrium (the Taylor principle). | False for negative `δ`: both roots can leave the unit circle through `−1`; `κ = γ = 1`, `β = 1/2`, `δ = −5` is determinate (`ex_2_2_a_printed_false`). Determinacy holds iff `δ > 1` or `δ < −1 − 2(1 + β)γ/κ` (`ex_2_2_a`), and for `δ ≥ 0` iff `δ > 1` (`ex_2_2_a_taylor_principle`). |
| Ex 2(b), p. 83 | Conditions for the Taylor rule. | With `δ_x ≥ 0`: iff `κ(δ_π − 1) + (1 − β)δ_x > 0` or `κ(1 + δ_π) + (1 + β)(2γ + δ_x) < 0` (`ex_2_2_b_exact`); for `δ_π, δ_x ≥ 0` the second branch is impossible and the condition is Bullard and Mitra's (`ex_2_2_b`). |
| p. 54 | Theorem 1.4.3 "also applies here". | As in Chapter 1 it is false as printed; the stable-solution versions are proved (`theorem_1_4_3_gt_stoch`, `theorem_1_4_3_lt_stoch`, `theorem_1_4_3_lt_exists_stoch`). They need a nonzero `F₀`-measurable random variable, which exists in `L¹` but not in every `CondExpSystem`. |

## Conventions and departures

| Where | Book | Lean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p. 45 | A probability space, a filtration and `E_t`. | The linear theory is proved for any `CondExpSystem`: continuous linear `E_t` with `‖E_t‖ ≤ 1` and the tower property. The conditional expectations of `L¹(Ω, F, Pr)` are an instance (`condExpSystemL1_real`, `condExpSystemL1_complex`), identified a.e. with `μ[·|F_t]` (`condExpSystemL1_ae_eq`). Random variables are `L¹` classes, so equalities hold almost surely. |
| Definition 2.3.1, p. 52 | `‖x‖_max = maxⱼ E|xⱼ|`. | A vector random variable is `ι → V`, stable means `‖x_{t,j}‖ < M`; with `V = L¹` this is the book's definition exactly (`l1_isStableProc_iff`). |
| Example 2.2.1, §2.2.2, Ex 1 | Shocks IID with mean zero, or white noise. | The proofs use `E_tε_{t+1} = 0` (a martingale difference with respect to the filtration), which IID-ness alone does not give unless the shocks are independent of the past, and white noise does not give at all. For Exercise 1 the closed form solves the model iff `E_tε_{t+1} = 0` (`ex_1_solution_iff`). The bursting bubble needs products of random variables and is proved with Mathlib's `μ[·|F_t]` (`example_2_2_1_bubble`) and transferred to `L¹` (`example_2_2_1_bubble_L1`). |
| (2.6), (2.10) | Equations involving `x_{t−1}`. | Imposed for `t ≥ 1` with `x₀` given. |
| §2.2.2, p. 52 | The display chaining the guess and the matching conditions. | The matching conditions are sufficient (`uc_sufficient`) and necessary under mild non-degeneracy (`uc_necessary`); the stable-root solution is the lag-operator solution (`uc_eq_lag_solution`). |
| §2.3.1, pp. 53–54 | Theorem 1.4.2 "still applies with small changes". | Solutions and `z` are adapted and `k₀` is `F₀`-measurable; the last is necessary (`bkS_k0_adapted`). Existence needs only a right inverse of `P_uy`, uniqueness only a left inverse (`bkS_exists`, `bkS_unique`). The printed (2.12) is right. |
| p. 54 | Certainty equivalence. | Three statements: with `E_t = id` the stochastic stable solutions are the deterministic ones (`certainty_equivalence`), the forward solutions agree (`certainty_equivalence_fwd`), and under `E_tz_{t+1} = Φz_t` the solution is the deterministic one with `Σ = 0` (`certainty_equivalence_ar_det`). |
| Example 2.3.1, pp. 54–55 | "`e_t` represents exogenous IID shocks"; parameters positive. | `e_t` means `ε_t` and `ε^x_t`. Only `κ, β, γ > 0` and `|ρ_r| < 1` are used; `β < 1` is not needed. The printed `W` is right. Indeterminacy is shown by free initial values along a stable direction (`example_2_3_1_indeterminate`), not by sunspot shocks. |
| p. 57 | The simplified Klein form under (2.1). | Needs only `E_tz_{t+1} = Φz_t` and `Φ` power-bounded, for the Sylvester equation (`klS_sylvester_exists`, `klein_simplified_form`). |
| Definition 2.3.4, p. 56 | `ξ_{t+1} = X_{t+1} − E_tX_{t+1}` is an exogenous martingale difference. | Any prediction error is a martingale difference (`predError_isMartingaleDiff`); the content is that `ξ` is given in advance. The stochastic Klein theorem also needs `ξ` stable and `F_{t+1}`-measurable. |
| p. 58 | The LQ first-order conditions. | Derived as first-variation identities of the date-`t` Lagrangian (`lq_variation_u`, `lq_variation_x`); the printed system is right for the multiplier convention `+β^{t+1}λ′_{t+1}(x_{t+1} − Ax_t − Bu_t − Cξ_{t+1})` (`lq_system_iff`). Passing from the pathwise conditions to `E_tλ_{t+1}` needs products of random variables and is not formalised. |
| p. 56 | A martingale difference process may not be stable. | Shown in an abstract `CondExpSystem` on `ℓ^∞` (truncations), not on a probability space (`example_mdiff_not_stable`). |
| pp. 56, 47 | White noise not satisfying (2.2); the stationary-law counterexample. | Built on infinite products of a finite uniform space (`Measure.infinitePi`); a finite space cannot carry infinitely many orthogonal nonzero variables. |
| Example 2.3.2, p. 64 | The Sims form of (2.16). | The printed matrices are right, with `y = (i, ξˣ, ξ^π, x, π)`, `ε = (ε, εˣ)`, `η = (ηˣ, η^π)`, for `t ≥ 1` (`example_2_3_2`). |
| p. 63 | The sunspot term `Q₁.Π(I − V.1V.1ᴴ)V.2e_t`. | The factor `I − V.1V.1ᴴ` is redundant (`sunspot_term_simplified`); the representation with `Θ₃` needs neither assumption (`sunspot_solution`). |
| (2.25), p. 62 | The SVD of `Q₂.Π`. | Proved to exist from the spectral theorem for `BᴴB`, with positive diagonal and `r = rank B`, together with `V.2` (`exists_thinSVD`); only `U.1` is constructed, as only it is used. The uniqueness conditions are also stated with ranks (`corollary_2_3_1_rank`, `lubik_schorfheide_rank`). |
| §2.4, pp. 65–69 | The scalar case; IID shocks with bounded support; `g`, `h` smooth. | `n_x = n_y = 1`, so `f` has two components and each step is a 2×2 system; the shock has a finite mean-zero distribution (`IsFiniteShock`), so the expectation is a finite sum; `f`, `g`, `h` are `C²` and `F ≡ 0` on all of `ℝ²` (`IsPertSolution`). Existence and smoothness of `g`, `h` is an assumption of the method. The cross coefficient of `pertTaylor2` is the `σ`-derivative of `g_x`. |
