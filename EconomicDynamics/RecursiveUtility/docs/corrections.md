# Corrections and departures: Miao Chapter 20

Jianjun Miao, *Economic Dynamics in Discrete Time* (draft of 21 April 2013), Chapter 20, Recursive
Utility, pp. 675–736. Lean names are relative to `Miao.RecursiveUtility`. Every book item is located
in the [source map](source-map.md).

## §§20.1–20.2.1 and Exercise 20.1

### Settings

1. **Consumption spaces.** Bundles lie in a set `K` of a real normed space `E` (the book: `ℝˡ₊`
   with the Euclidean norm). The book's `L` (bounded streams, sup norm) and `ℓ^∞_θ` are encoded as
   sets of bounded sequences `b` with `c_t = θᵗb_t ∈ K` (`ku_Lθ`, `ku_seqθ`); the encoding is
   isometric for the `θ`-norm (`ku_thetaNorm_seqθ`), onto (`ku_Lθ_surj`), and `θ = 1` is `L`
   (`ku_seqθ_one`). `θ > 0` replaces `θ ≥ 1`. "`ℓ^∞_θ` is a Banach space" is read as completeness
   for closed `K` (`ku_Lθ_complete`), since `ℝˡ₊` is not a vector space. §20.1.1's algebra uses any
   set of streams closed under shift and prepending (`ku_bddSeq_closed` for the book's `L`).
2. **Uncertainty.** §20.2 uses the book's finite-state Markov setting: paths `ω : ℕ → S`, `F_t`
   generated by `ω₀, …, ω_t`, fixed `s₀` (so `F₀` is trivial on paths from `s₀`); "almost surely"
   and "with positive probability" refer to histories of positive probability (`ku_Reach`).
   Definition 20.2.3 is stated for random variables with values in a set `D` (the power
   certainty equivalents need `D = (0, ∞)`), with continuity in the topology of pointwise
   convergence. Definition 20.2.1 is formalised as printed; it only expresses independence of
   past consumption, not of unrealized consumption on other branches.

### §20.1.1

3. **"Any preference order with a recursive representation satisfies Koopmans's Axioms"
   (p. 678)** is false. (K1) holds when `W` is strictly increasing in its second argument
   (`ku_K1_of_recursive`), (K2) when it is strictly increasing in its first (`ku_K2_of_recursive`),
   and then (K3) holds iff `u` is not constant (`ku_K3_iff_of_recursive`). Counterexamples:
   `U(c) = c₀` (`W(a, y) = a`) violates (K1) (`ku_K1_counterexample`); `U ≡ 0` violates (K3)
   (`ku_K3_counterexample`).
4. **Koopmans (1960) (p. 678)** is proved for the existence of some `W` from (K1)–(K2)
   (`ku_koopmans_1960`); (K3) is not needed and the regularity of `W` is not addressed.
5. **Dynamic consistency.** "Axiom (K1) ensures dynamic consistency" is proved
   (`ku_dynConsistent_of_K1`), as is dynamic consistency of (20.1) with `W` strictly increasing
   in `U` (`ku_dynConsistent_of_recursive`).
6. **EIS sign (p. 679).** With the printed definition `EIS = d ln(c_s/c_t)/d ln MRS_{s,t}`, the
   CES aggregator of Example 20.1.2 gives `−1/ρ` (`example_20_1_2_misprint`); the stated `1/ρ` is
   `d ln(c_t/c_s)/d ln MRS_{s,t}` (`example_20_1_2`). The partial derivatives are computed from the
   recursion itself (`example_20_1_2_mrs`), for any positive solution `U`.

### §20.1.2

7. **Theorem 20.1.1** is proved with `0 ≤ β < 1`, (W1) used only at `z = 0`, and uniqueness among
   all bounded continuous solutions (`theorem_20_1_1`). Concavity needs only that `W` be increasing
   in its second argument together with (W4) (`theorem_20_1_1_concave`).
8. **(W5) (p. 681)** as printed, `|W(x, y) − W(x, y₀)| < δ|y − y₀|` for all `y, y₀ ∈ ℝ₊`, fails for
   every `W` at `y = y₀` (`ku_W5_misprint`). It is used with `≤` and for all `y, y₀ ∈ ℝ` (solutions
   may take negative values).
9. **Theorem 20.1.2 hypotheses.** The printed "(W2) and (W5)" makes (W5) redundant; existence,
   uniqueness and `T_Wⁿ0 → U` need only (W5) (`theorem_20_1_2`, via a direct contraction,
   `ku_TW_contraction`). The book's proof, through Theorem 7.3.6, also uses that `W` is increasing
   in `y` (W3); that route is `theorem_20_1_2_via_7_3_6`.
10. **Strict concavity in Theorem 20.1.2** ("strictly [concave] in `x` and strictly increasing in
    `y`"; the word "concave" is missing) is proved under joint concavity of `W` and strict
    concavity in the direction of `x`, `aW(x, y) + bW(x', y') < W(ax + bx', ay + by')` for
    `x ≠ x'` (`theorem_20_1_2_strict`). Strict concavity in `x` for fixed `y` does not by itself
    give the needed strict inequality when `y ≠ y'`.
11. **The example on p. 682.** `c₀^{1−γ} < |c|_θ^{1−γ}` should be `≤` (it fails at `c = 0`,
    `ku_example_682_misprint`; corrected `ku_example_682_W0`). The conclusion
    `β sup ϕ(₁c)/ϕ(c) < 1` does not follow from the pointwise `βϕ(₁c) < ϕ(c)`
    (`ku_pointwise_not_sup`); it follows from the uniform bound
    `βϕ(₁c) ≤ max(β, βθ^{1−γ})ϕ(c)` (`ku_example_682_ratio`, `ku_example_682_sup`). The utility is
    then the discounted sum (`ku_example_682_series`).

### §20.2.1

12. **Dynamic consistency of (20.2)** (asserted, p. 684) is proved for `W` strictly increasing in
    its second argument and `M` monotone and strictly monotone on successors of positive
    probability (`ku_dynConsistent_of_eq_20_2`); Epstein–Zin utility with positive values is an
    instance (`ku_ez_dynConsistent`).
13. **Time-additive expected utility (p. 685).** `E_t[∑_{s≥t} β^{s−t}u(c_s)]` is represented as
    `∑_k βᵏE_t[u(c_{t+k})]`, with the conditional expectation of a date-`t + k` variable the
    iterated one-step expectation (`ku_Eiter`); the identity is proved for bounded `u(c)` and
    bounded solutions (`ku_additive_sum`, `ku_timeAdditive_eu_sum`, `ku_timeAdditive_R`).
14. **Existence, uniqueness and homotheticity of (20.3).** The book asserts homotheticity without
    an existence result. Proved: for stationary Markov consumption and irreducible `P`, a unique
    positive Markov solution (`ku_ez_markov`) that is homogeneous of degree one
    (`ku_ez_markov_homogeneous`); with a finite horizon, homogeneity of `U_t` (`ku_ezFin_homog`).
15. **(20.5).** The limit `ρ → 1` is taken in the aggregator (`ku_ces_limit`, needs `0 ≤ β ≤ 1`),
    and (20.5) is derived for the limiting Cobb–Douglas aggregator (`eq_20_5`), not as a limit of
    solutions.
16. **Certainty equivalent (p. 688).** "If `U` is strictly increasing, then there is a unique `μ`":
    uniqueness holds (`ku_ce_unique`), existence needs continuity along constants
    (`ku_ce_exists`); without it `μ` may not exist (`ku_ce_counterexample`).
17. **Allais display (p. 687)** "`u(1M) ≥ 0.1u(5M) + 0.89u(1M) ⟺ 0.11u(1M) + 0.1u(5M)`" lacks the
    inequality; it is `⟺ 0.11u(1M) ≥ 0.1u(5M)` (`ku_allais_u0`; general `u(0)`: `ku_allais`).
18. **Property (iv) (p. 689)** `M(kc, m) = kM(c, m)` is false for the expected-utility aggregator
    (`ku_euM_iv_misprint`); linear homogeneity is `M(kc, km) = kM(c, m)` (`ku_euM_iv`).
19. **Weighted utility parameters (p. 689).** "For `M` to be increasing and concave in `c` in a
    neighborhood of `m`, the parameters must satisfy (i) or (ii)": at `c = m`, `∂M/∂c = 1` and
    `∂²M/∂c² = (α + 2γ − 1)/m` (`ku_wM_local`), so (i) or (ii) (each implying `α + 2γ < 1`,
    `ku_wM_params`) suffices, but neither is necessary: `γ = 0`, `α = 1/2` gives an increasing
    concave aggregator (`ku_wM_params_not_necessary`).
20. **Disappointment aversion (p. 690).** The printed `p̂(s) = (1 + δ1(c(s) < μ))/(1 + δ∑ p 1)`
    omits the factor `p(s)` (`ku_gulM_hat_misprint`); with it, `μ^α = ∑ p̂ c^α` holds
    (`ku_gulM_ce_hat`, for `δ ≥ 0`).

### Exercise 20.1

21. **Exercise 20.1 (p. 735)** claims `U_t` is strictly concave for `t < T` when `ρ, γ > 0`. This
    is false: `U_t` is homogeneous of degree one, hence linear along rays, and is never strictly
    concave (`ex_20_1_not_strict`). What holds is concavity, for `0 < β < 1`, `ρ ≥ 0`, `γ ≥ 0`,
    `ρ, γ ≠ 1` (`ex_20_1`). Definition 20.2.1 holds for the dates `t ≤ T` only
    (`ku_ezFin_dynUtility`, `ku_ezFin_meas`).

## §20.2.2–§20.3 and Exercises 20.2–20.3

### Setting

22. **Finite event tree, finite horizon (departure).** The book's dynamic models ((20.7), the
   recursive divergence utility, (20.10)–(20.19), Proposition 20.3.2, Examples 20.3.1–20.3.3) live
   on an infinite horizon over a filtration generated by a finite Markov chain. Here they are on a
   finite event tree with finitely many shocks per period (histories `List S`, one-step
   distributions `p h`, so Markov transitions are the case `p h s = π(s | head h)`) and a finite
   horizon with terminal utility `U_T = c_T` (or `u(c_T)`): `aa_ezU`, `aa_rmpU`, `aa_rdU`,
   `aa_juU`, `aa_recU`, `aa_kmm09U`. No existence-by-contraction argument is needed.

### §20.2.2 Ambiguity aversion

23. **Rectangularity (p. 693).** The book states that rectangularity of `P` makes
   `min_P E^P[∑ βᵗu(c_t)]` satisfy (20.7). Proved for rectangular sets generated node by node, with
   finitely many priors at each node so the minimum is attained (`aa_rmpU_isLeast_selection`,
   two periods: `aa_rectangular_recursion`); that the failure can occur is shown by a
   non-rectangular two-prior set whose minimum `1/2` differs from the recursive value `0`
   (`aa_nonrectangular_counterexample`).
24. **Relative entropy (p. 695).** `D_φ¹(q‖p) = ∫ ln(dq/dp) dq` uses `dq/dp = q/p`, which needs
   `q ≪ p`; it is proved for a full-support reference model (`aa_phiDiv_kl`), and the multiplier
   minimisation over `q ≪ p` for any `p` (`aa_multiplier_isLeast`).
25. **Monotone mean–variance (pp. 695–696)** is proved with `u(c) = c` and a full-support reference
   model; the minimiser is the density `1 − (c − Ec)/θ`, nonnegative exactly on the book's domain
   `c − Ec ≤ θ` (`aa_chi2_mmv`).
26. **Recursive divergence utility (p. 696).** The book writes `min` over densities; for a general
   `φ` the minimum need not be attained, so `R_t` is an infimum (`aa_divR`) and dynamic consistency
   is the statement that the recursive utility is the greatest lower bound of the time-zero formula
   over all adapted distortion processes (`aa_rdU_isGLB`, for `0 ≤ β ≤ 1`). For relative entropy
   the infimum is attained and `R_t` is the risk-sensitive operator (`aa_divR_kl`,
   `aa_klDensity_attains`). With a finite horizon the last period carries no penalty
   (`aa_rdUm_eq_sum`).
27. **Ambiguity aversion iff `η > γ` (p. 699)** is proved in the form: `η ≥ γ` lowers and `η ≤ γ`
   raises the certainty equivalent (20.15) relative to the ambiguity-neutral (Epstein–Zin) value,
   and (20.15) and (20.14) are decreasing in `η` (`aa_ju_le_KP`, `aa_KP_le_ju`, `aa_ju_antitone`,
   `aa_W_ju_antitone`), from the power-mean inequality (`aa_powMean_mono`).
28. **(20.16).** The limit `ρ → 1` is the convergence of the CES aggregator to `c^{1−β}y^β`
   (`aa_W_tendsto_rho_one`); (20.16) is then the exact identity for `V = ln U`
   (`eq_20_16`).
29. **(20.18).** The limit `η → ∞` of (20.17) is `min_z E_{π_z}V` only over hidden states with
   positive posterior probability; the theorem assumes the minimising state has `μ(z*) > 0`
   (`eq_20_18`).
30. **Footnote 2 (p. 699).** Only the "if" direction is proved: with `θ₁ = θ₂` the multiplier model
   with hidden states is risk-sensitive (Kreps–Porteus–Epstein–Zin) under the predictive distribution
   (`aa_smoothM_exp_equal`). Not formalised: "only when `θ₁ = θ₂`".
31. **Ordinal equivalence with (20.19) (p. 700).** With a finite horizon the transform
    `V = U^{1−γ}/((1 − γ)(1 − β))` satisfies (20.19) at every node when the terminal utility of
    (20.19) is `c_T^{1−γ}/((1 − γ)(1 − β))` (`aa_juMiao_ordinal`; the one-step identity is
    `aa_juMiao_step`).
32. **Not formalised (cited without derivation):** Choquet expected utility (footnote 1); the
    probabilistic sophistication of divergence preferences with `w = 1` and their relation to the
    Ellsberg paradox (Maccheroni, Marinacci and Rustichini 2006a); the remark that the exponential
    specification lacks homogeneity.

### §20.2.3 Temporal resolution of uncertainty

33. **Theorem 20.2.1 (p. 704): "convex in `y`".** Only the values `y = E_m U₁` matter: the
    theorem is proved with convexity (concavity) of `W(x, ·)` on the convex set
    `Y = {E_m U₁ : m ∈ M(X)}`, for `U₁` bounded and measurable and `x` ranging over `X`
    (`theorem_20_2_1`, `theorem_20_2_1_late`). Definition 20.2.4 is the weak ranking over the two
    programs (`definition_20_2_4`).
34. **Epstein–Zin prefers earlier resolution "iff `γ > ρ`" (p. 704, Exercise 20.3).** With the
    (weak) preference of Definition 20.2.4 the condition is `γ ≥ ρ` (`ex_20_3`, for consumption in
    `[a, b]`, `0 < a < b`, `ρ, γ ≠ 1`, `β ∈ (0, 1)`): at `γ = ρ` the agent is indifferent
    (`aa_ez_indifferent`). The book's `γ > ρ` characterises strict preference for every pair of
    lotteries with different expected utilities (`aa_ez_strictEarly`), and `γ < ρ` gives strict
    preference for later resolution on two Dirac lotteries (inside the proof of `ex_20_3`). The
    two-period Epstein–Zin utility is written in the Kreps–Porteus form
    `W̃(c, y) = u(W(c, u⁻¹(y)))` (`aa_ezKP`, `aa_ezKP_eq`).

### §20.3.1 Concavity

35. **"Strictly increasing concave `W` and strictly concave `M` give strictly concave `U`" (p. 705)
    is false.** With `W(c, y) = c + βy` and any `M`, perturbing consumption at one node moves
    `U_{n+1}` affinely (`aa_recU_not_strictConcave`). The weak version holds by backward induction
    (`aa_recU_concaveOn`).
36. **"`U_t` is strictly concave" for Epstein–Zin with `ρ, γ > 0` (p. 705) is false.** `U_t` is
    homogeneous of degree one (`aa_ezU_smul`), hence affine on rays and never strictly concave
    (`aa_ezU_not_strictConcave`). It is concave (`aa_ezU_concaveOn`, for `ρ, γ > 0`, `ρ, γ ≠ 1`).
37. **Lemma 20.3.1 (p. 705).** (ii) is printed with `f(λx + (1 − λ))`; read `f(λx + (1 − λ)y)`
    (`aa_LevelCond`). (i) ⇔ (iii) holds as stated (`lemma_20_3_1_i_iff_iii`) and (i) ⇒ (ii) holds
    (`lemma_20_3_1_i_ii`), but (ii) ⇒ (i), (iii) needs `f > 0` (Skiadas' setting), under which the
    three are equivalent (`lemma_20_3_1`). Counterexample without positivity:
    `f(x) = φ(x₁ − x₂)`, `φ(s) = s + max(s, 0)`, satisfies (ii) but not (iii) or (i)
    (`lemma_20_3_1_counterexample`).

### §20.3.2 Risk aversion

38. **Risk aversion (p. 706) as printed is never satisfied:** the condition must hold for `c = b`,
    which gives `U¹_t(b) > U¹_t(b)` (`aa_riskAverse_never`). Even restricted to random `c`
    (`aa_RiskAverse'`), "risk averse if it is more risk averse than a risk-neutral `U²`" fails: the
    risk-neutral utility is more risk averse than itself but not risk averse
    (`aa_moreRiskAverse_not_riskAverse`).
39. **Proposition 20.3.1, first part.** Proved with `W` strictly increasing in its second argument,
    certainty equivalents with `M(k) = k` and `M¹` monotone (`proposition_20_3_1`); the book's
    "ordinally equivalent over deterministic plans" is automatic (`aa_recU_deterministic`).
40. **Proposition 20.3.1, second part ("standard risk theory").** Pratt's theorem is proved on an
    interval `D` for continuous strictly increasing `u_i` (`proposition_20_3_1_pratt`). The converse
    (more risk averse ⇒ `u₁ = φ ∘ u₂`, `φ` concave) needs the certainty-equivalent comparison for
    all two-point lotteries, which the first part delivers only for the conditional distributions
    of the given tree; the direction "`φ` concave ⇒ more risk averse" holds for every tree
    (`proposition_20_3_1_ez`, for `u_i` order isomorphisms of `ℝ`).

### §20.3.3 Utility gradients and pricing kernels

41. **Definition 20.3.2 (p. 707) as printed only says that `M` is continuous:** the condition
    `E h² < δ ⇒ |M(z + h) − M(z) − E[κh]| < ε` holds for `M = E` with both `κ = 1` and `κ = 0`
    (`aa_CEDerivPrinted_not_unique`). The intended notion is Fréchet differentiability, the error
    being `o(‖h‖)` (`aa_HasCEDeriv`), under which `κ = 1` is the derivative of `E` and `κ = 0` is
    not (`aa_HasCEDeriv_expectation`, `aa_not_HasCEDeriv_expectation_zero`). In the illustration
    "the derivative of `v` exists" and `u'(v_t(x_{t+1}))` should read `M` and `u'(M_t(x_{t+1}))`
    (`aa_euCE_hasCEDeriv`).
42. **Proposition 20.3.2 (p. 708).** With a finite horizon the condition
    `lim E[π_t x_t] = 0` is not needed, and the terminal `λ_T = 1` because `U_T = c_T`
    (`aa_gradπ`, `proposition_20_3_2`, `proposition_20_3_2_gradient`). In the proof,
    `W₂(t) = W(c_t, M_t(U_{t+1}(c)))` should read `W₂(t) = W₂(c_t, M_t(U_{t+1}(c)))`. The
    hypotheses (`aa_GradHyp`) are verified for Epstein–Zin (`example_20_3_1`).
43. **Marginal value of wealth (pp. 708–709)** is proved as an envelope theorem on a normed space
    under a binding budget and the first-order condition `L = μB` (`aa_marginal_value_wealth`,
    `aa_lambda0_eq_mu`).
44. **Example 20.3.3 (p. 710).** The supergradient is `π_t = βᵗu'(c_t)M*_t` with `M*_t` the product
    of the one-step densities `m* = dq*/dp` of minimising priors; the book's `π_t = u'(c_t)m*_t`
    leaves the discounting and the cumulation implicit. Only the inclusion "every such process is a
    supergradient" is proved (`example_20_3_3`, `example_20_3_3_exists`), for concave `u` with
    supergradient `u'` and a full-support reference model. Not formalised: the reverse inclusion,
    cited from Epstein and Wang (1994).
45. **Exercise 20.2, divergence utility.** Proved for relative entropy (the multiplier case), where
    `R_t` is differentiable with derivative the minimising density (`aa_riskSensitive_hasCEDeriv`,
    `ex_20_2_divergence`); for a general divergence the minimising density is a supergradient of
    `R_t` (`aa_divR_supergradient`).

## §20.4–§20.5 and Exercises 20.4–20.7

### §20.4.1

46. **Setting.** Conditional expectations at date `t` are finite sums over next-period states
   (`pa_E`); risky assets are indexed by a finite type and the riskless asset carries the
   remaining weight (`pa_Rw`, equal to the book's `∑ψᵢRᵢ` with `∑ψᵢ = 1` by
   `pa_wealthReturn_eq`). The first-order conditions (20.22) and the portfolio condition are
   proved as necessary conditions at an interior local maximum (`eq_20_22`,
   `pa_portfolio_foc`); the text does not discuss sufficiency. The conjecture `J_t = A_t X_t` is
   verified for the supremum over consumption ratios and attainable portfolio returns
   (`pa_value_homogeneous`).

### §20.4.2

47. **(20.32): the last variance term is squared.** The printed `½ (ρ−γ)/(1−ρ) σ_w²` should be
   `½ ((ρ−γ)/(1−ρ))² σ_w²` (as in (20.34)); with the square, (20.33) follows (`eq_20_32`,
   `eq_20_33`). The book writes `σ_c, σ_w` for variances and then `σ_c², σ_w²`; Lean uses the
   covariance matrix entries `V 0 0`, `V 1 1` throughout.
48. **Lognormality made explicit.** "Conditionally lognormal and homoskedastic" is the hypothesis
   that `(Δc, r_w, r_i)` is jointly Gaussian under the conditional law (`pa_JointGaussian`, with
   a concrete instance `pa_jointGaussian_rank_one`); the formula `ln E X = E ln X + ½ Var ln X`
   is `pa_log_integral_exp_gaussian`.
49. **(20.36) is an approximation; its error is formalised.** The exact log return is
   `d' − p + ln(1 + e^{p'−d'})` (`pa_cs_exact`); (20.36) is its tangent at `p̄ − d̄`, with error
   `o(p' − d' − (p̄ − d̄))` (`eq_20_36`). The later results use (20.36) as an exact identity, as
   the text does.
50. **Terminal condition of (20.37).** The text imposes `lim ϱⁱ p_{t+i} = 0`; the forward
   solution uses `ϱⁿ(p_{t+n} − d_{t+n}) → 0`, which is what `eq_20_37` assumes. (20.37) is proved
   path by path and then in conditional expectation (`eq_20_37_cond`); (20.39)–(20.41) use linear
   conditional-expectation operators, and (20.40)–(20.41) a finite sample space so that `E_t`
   commutes with the limits of the discounted sums.
51. **The real consol is `λ = 0`, not `λ = 1`.** The text says "by setting `λ = 1` in equations
   (20.42) and (20.43)" for the consol yield and return; the printed formulas are the `λ = 0`
   case (as the text itself states on p. 718). Proved for `λ = 0`: `pa_consol_yield`,
   `pa_consol_news`; the `λ = 1` formula differs (`pa_consol_lambda_one_counterexample`).
52. **The wealth-return decomposition (p. 720).** "Equation (20.35) holds for wealth portfolio
   `λ = 1`" refers to (20.41). The revision sum in the wealth news and in `σ_ig` starts at
   `j = 1` and is discounted by `ϱʲ` (the text writes `∑_{j=0}^∞` and `ρⁱ`); with `j = 0`
   included, current consumption news is counted twice and `σ_iw = σ_ic + (1 − ρ)σ_ig` fails.
   `pa_sigma_iw`, `pa_sum_revision`, `pa_premium_gamma`, `pa_lrr_equity_premium`.

### §20.4.3

53. **Shocks.** The IID `N(0,1)` shocks are independent random variables with Gaussian laws under
   the time-`t` conditional probability (`pa_stdNormal_pi` gives an instance); the state is
   `(x_t, σ_t)` with `σ_t > 0` (the Gaussian volatility process of the model can make `σ_t²`
   negative; the text ignores this, and so do the formulas). The equity Campbell–Shiller constant
   is `κ₁ₑ` throughout: the text mixes `κ₁` and `κ₁ₑ` in (20.55) and in `β_{e,e}, β_{e,w}`, and
   writes `κ₁A₂ₑσ_t²` for `κ₁ₑA₂ₑσ²_{t+1}` (`eq_20_55`).
54. **The market price of volatility risk is negative.** The text states `λ_{m,w} =
   (γ − ρ)/(1 − ρ) A₂κ₁ > 0` and "the last two terms are positive". Since `A₂ < 0` when `γ > 1`
   and `ρ < 1` (as the text proves on p. 725), `λ_{m,w} < 0` under exactly those conditions
   (`pa_by_lam_w_neg`; counterexample to the printed claim: `pa_by_lam_w_not_pos`). The sign of
   the volatility-risk contribution `κ₁A₂λ_{m,w}σ_w²` in (20.53) is nevertheless positive.
55. **"An increase in `ν₁` raises `A₂`"** should read "raises `|A₂|`": `A₂` becomes more
    negative (`pa_by_A2_magnified`).
56. **(20.53), (20.56) and `Q₁, Q₂`: notation.** `Var_t(r_{a,t+1})` in (20.53) is
    `Var_t(r_{w,t+1})`; `λ_{e,e}, λ_{e,w}` in (20.56) and `λ_{e,η}, λ_{e,e}` in `Q₁, Q₂` are

57. **The unconditional equity return has `+ρx_t`.** The text writes
    `r_{e,t+1} − E r_{e,t+1} = −ρx_t + …`; with `A₁ₑ` from (20.57) the coefficient of `x_t` is

58. **The riskfree-rate equation (p. 729).** From `E_t[MR_f] = 1`, the coefficient of

### §20.5

59. **Lemma 20.5.1 holds for the comprehensive set.** Free disposal fails for the plain utility
    possibility set `U₀(e)` (one agent, `U(c) = c`, `e = 1`: `−1 ≤ 0 ∈ U₀(e)` but
    `−1 ∉ U₀(e)`; `lemma_20_5_1_counterexample`). The lemma is proved for
    `Ū₀(e) = {u : u ≤ U(c), c ∈ C(e)}` under concave, continuous utilities and `e ≥ 0`
    (hypotheses the text leaves implicit), with consumption plans in a finite-dimensional space
    (`lemma_20_5_1`); `U₀(e)` itself is compact (`lemma_20_5_1_plain_closed`). The Negishi value
    is unchanged by the hull (`pa_Vhat_U0bar`).
60. **Lemma 20.5.2 holds for the comprehensive set.** With `U₀(e)`, `v = −1` in the example above
    satisfies (20.62) but `v ∉ U₀(e)` (`lemma_20_5_2_counterexample`). The corrected lemma is
    `lemma_20_5_2`, proved, as in the text, from the separation theorem (`pa_mem_iff_support`).
    `min_θ` is read as `∀ θ ∈ Δⁿ` (the infimum need not be attained in the abstract lemma).
61. **Proposition 20.5.1.** Proved in a one-step recursive economy (date-0 consumption,
    continuation economies in each next-period state) with `Wⁱ` increasing in the continuation
    value and `Rⁱ` monotone (implicit in the text): the Negishi and Bellman problems have the
    same upper bounds and the same supremum (`proposition_20_5_1`); "max" is read as "sup". The
    text's third equality (continuations of Pareto optima are Pareto optimal) is not needed.
62. **Proposition 20.5.2 needs positive weights.** A planner solution with a zero weight need not
    be Pareto optimal (`proposition_20_5_2_counterexample`: agent 0 indifferent, weight
    `(1, 0)`, nothing consumed). Proved: an allocation generated by the dynamic program — a
    maximiser `(c₀, v₁)` of (20.60) whose continuation allocation delivers the promised
    utilities `v₁` — solves the Negishi problem (20.58), and is Pareto optimal when all weights
    are positive (`proposition_20_5_2`). (The proof's `c'_t` is a misprint for `c̃_t`.)
63. **Dumas–Wang–Uppal duality.** `W(c, u) = min_ν F(c, ν) + (1 − ν)u` holds where `W(c, ·)` has a
    supergradient (e.g. concave and differentiable in `u`), the minimum being over the `ν` for
    which `F(c, ν) < ∞` and attained at `ν = 1 − W_u(c, u)` (`pa_dwu_duality`); the planner
    recursion is proved as a one-step identity (`pa_dwu_planner`).
