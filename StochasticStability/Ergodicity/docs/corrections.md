# Corrections and conventions

This file lists the places where a Lean statement differs from the book: a claim corrected, a
hypothesis made explicit, a proof replaced, or a convention chosen. Four of the claims below are
refuted in Lean by a counterexample, and for each of them a corrected statement is proved. Three
of the counterexamples are in this project; the fourth, to Theorem 13.0.1 (iii), is carried from
Chapter 11. For the other entries the table says why no counterexample is given. The last section
lists what is not formalised.

## Claims corrected

| Proposition 13.1.1, p. 318 | For a positive Harris chain on a countable space, "for any `x, y, α ∈ X`", `|Pⁿ(x, y) − π(y)| ≤ _αPⁿ(x, y) + |a_x ∗ u − π(α)| ∗ t_y (n) + π(α) Σ_{j > n} t_y(j)` (13.17). | The proof uses the representation (13.13), `π(y) = π(α) Σ_j t_y(j)`, which holds for `α ∈ B⁺(X)` and fails for a transient `α`. It is positive Harris for `ψ = δ₁ + δ₂` (`oscPsi`, `oscKernel_isHarris`), with invariant probability `π = ½δ₁ + ½δ₂` (`oscPi`, `oscPi_isInvariant`). The state `α = 0` has `ψ{0} = 0`. The proposition is proved for `ψ{α} > 0` (`proposition_13_1_1`), which is what the book's definition of an ergodic atom (13.22) requires. That proof needs only positivity and measurable singletons, not the Harris property or a countable space. |
| Proposition 13.2.4 (ii), p. 323 | "If `Φ` is a positive recurrent aperiodic Markov chain on a countable space, then for every initial state `x`, `‖Pⁿ(x, ·) − π‖ → 0`" (13.38). | False (`proposition_13_2_4_ii_counterexample`). The leaky chain `leakKernel` on `ℕ` has `0` absorbing, and from `i ≥ 1` it moves to `0` with probability `2^{−(i+1)}` (`leakProb`) and to `i + 1` otherwise. It is positive with `ψ = π = δ₀` (`leakKernel_isPositive`, `isInvariant_leakKernel`), hence recurrent, and aperiodic (`leakKernel_isAperiodic`). From `1` the chain never reaches `0` with probability at least `1/2`, so `‖Pⁿ(1, ·) − δ₀‖ ≥ 1/2` for every `n` (`leakKernel_hitProb_zero`, `leakKernel_pow_zero`, `leakKernel_pow_le`). The proof in the book passes through Theorem 13.1.2, which assumes a positive Harris chain. The statement holds for positive Harris chains (`proposition_13_2_4_ii`), in particular when the chain is recurrent and all states communicate (`isHarris_of_countable`, carried), and for a positive chain from every state that reaches an accessible state almost surely (`proposition_13_2_4_ii_of_reach`), in particular from every `x` with `ψ{x} > 0`. |
| Theorem 13.3.1, p. 326 | "If `Φ` is a positive Harris recurrent and strongly aperiodic chain, then for any initial measure `λ`, `‖λPⁿ − π‖ → 0`" (13.53). With a minorization on a set `C` with `ψ(C) > 0` and `ν(C) > 0`, the theorem is proved (`theorem_13_3_1_ii`); part (i), for an accessible ergodic atom, is `theorem_13_3_1_i`. Aperiodic chains have such minorizations for a skeleton (`exists_skeleton_minorization`), which is how Theorem 13.3.3 uses it. |

## Conventions

* Transition laws are Mathlib's kernels, and `Pⁿ` is the kernel power. `μP` is `P ∘ₘ μ`. Mathlib's
  composition `η ∘ₖ κ` is "first `κ`, then `η`". Measures and kernels take values in `[0, ∞]`
  (`ℝ≥0∞`), and the decompositions of §§13.1–13.2 are turned into real statements with `toReal`.
* `tvNorm μ ν` is (13.7) for the signed measure `μ − ν`, a real number, used for finite `μ`, `ν`.
  `tvSup μ ν` is `sup_A |μ(A) − ν(A)|`. The book's `‖λPⁿ − π‖` is
  `tvNorm ((P ^ n) ∘ₘ λ) π`, and (13.4) is stated with `tvSup`. Sums of total variation norms,
  as in (13.75) and (13.81), are taken in `[0, ∞]` through `ENNReal.ofReal` (Mathlib).
* Sequences on `ℤ₊` are `ℕ → ℝ` and `a ∗ b` is `realConv a b`. An increment law (`IsIncrementLaw`)
  has `p(0) = 0`; a delay law (`IsDelayLaw`) may have `a(0) > 0`. `incrMean p` is `Σ n p(n)` when
  the series converges and `0` otherwise, and Lean's `0⁻¹ = 0`, so `renewal_theorem` states
  `u(n) → m_p⁻¹` in both cases. `theorem_13_2_3` keeps the book's hypothesis `m_p < ∞`, which its
  proof does not use. `Var(u)` is `renewalVar p`, and `u(−1) = 0` is built into `seqDiff`.
* The atom of §§13.1–13.2 is a measurable set `α` with a point `a ∈ α`. `tabooKernel P hα n x`
  (carried) is `_αPⁿ⁺¹(x, ·)` and `tabooSeq P hα λ n` (carried) is `λ _αPⁿ⁺¹`, so the
  decompositions are stated at time `n + 1`, that is for `n ≥ 1`. `entryLaw` and `lastExitSeq`
  vanish at `0`, as `a_λ(0) = t_f(0) = 0` in the book. Sums over `n ≥ 1`, as in (13.5), (13.75),
  (13.81) and (13.83), are written `Σ_{n ≥ 0}` of the terms at `n + 1`.
* The forward recurrence time chain `fwdRecKernel` (carried) of an increment law has its renewal
  state at `0`, and the linked chain is `linkedFwdRec (lawMeasure p)` (carried) on `ℕ × ℕ`. A
  common renewal is a visit to `(0, 0)`, the book's state `(1, 1)` in (13.30) and (13.68).
* A minorization `M : Minorization P` (carried) carries the set `M.C`, the constant `M.δ` and the
  probability `M.ν`. The split kernel is `M.splitKernel` on `X × Bool`, its atom `α̌` is
  `M.checkAlpha`, and the split measure `λ*` is `M.split λ`.
* Generating functions are `genFunC a z = Σ a(n) zⁿ` for complex `z`, and `genSeries a` is the
  same series as a `FormalMultilinearSeries` (Mathlib). "Analytic" is `DifferentiableOn ℂ` (Mathlib)
  on an open disc.
* The carried modules are copied from `InvariantMeasures` and `DriftRegularity` without change
  apart from the namespace, the imports, and a provenance paragraph in the docstrings of the five
  Chapter 11 modules (see the [README](../README.md)). The corrections they contain belong to
  Chapters 1–11, are recorded in their module docstrings and in those projects, and are not
  repeated here, except for the one to Theorem 11.3.14, which refutes Theorem 13.0.1 (iii) as
  printed.

* **§13.5**, the commentary (pp. 334–335).
* **The coupled renewal processes** (pp. 320–322, 330). The indicator processes `Z`, `Z_a`, `Z_b`
  and `Z_ab` and the coupling time `T_ab` as random variables on one probability space, with
  (13.30), (13.32) for general delays, (13.36) and (13.67). Proposition 13.2.1 is stated for the
  linked chain, and the renewal theorems are proved analytically.
* **The last-exit half of (13.39) for a general set `A`** (p. 324). The first-entrance half is
  proved for every measurable `A`, and the last-exit decomposition on atoms, which is all that
  (13.40) and the chapter use.
* **The continuous-time statements of §14.5** (pp. 354–358): Theorems 14.5.1 and 14.5.2 and
  Propositions 14.5.3–14.5.5 for a renewal measure on `ℝ₊`, with (14.37)–(14.50), the forward
  recurrence time `δ`-skeleton, and Theorem 14.5.2 (ii)–(iii). They belong to Chapter 14.
  Theorem 14.5.1 is only quoted from Feller in the book. Their discrete forms on `ℤ₊` are proved
  here.
* **The Markov chain results of §15.1** (pp. 364–371): (15.8)–(15.12), the geometrically ergodic,
  Kendall and `f`-Kendall atoms of §15.1.3, Proposition 15.1.2 for an atom (its renewal content is
  `kendall_geometric_bound`), Proposition 15.1.3, Theorems 15.1.4–15.1.5 and the examples of §15.1.4.
  They belong to Chapter 15, which can use `theorem_15_1_1`, `kendall_geometric_bound` and
  `geometric_delayed_bound`.
* **Informal remarks.** The opening of the chapter (p. 313); the four ideas of Part III, with
  independence of initial distributions and the role of renewal theory and splitting
  (pp. 314–316); the extension to general spaces through the split skeleton (p. 320); the
  reliance on the equivalence of positivity and regularity (p. 323); unbounded `f` and
  `f`-regularity (p. 326); the regularity of `Γ_e` (p. 357); and the remark on a coupling proof of
  Kendall's theorem (p. 368).
