# Corrections and conventions

This file lists the places where a Lean statement differs from the book: a claim corrected, a
hypothesis made explicit, a proof replaced, or a convention chosen. Six of the claims below are
refuted in Lean by a counterexample, and for each of them a corrected statement is proved. For the
other entries the table says why no counterexample is given. The last section lists what is not
formalised.

## Claims corrected

| Where | Book | Lean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theorem 9.3.6, p. 213 | For a ψ-irreducible T-chain, `X = H ∪ N` with `H` a maximal Harris set (or empty) and `N` transient, `R ⊆ H`, "and every state in `N` is topologically transient". The proof shows that `O_δ = {T(·, H) > δ}` is uniformly transient for `x* ∈ N_H = {y ∈ N : T(y, H) > 0}`. | `O_δ` may meet `H`, and then it is not uniformly transient. The chain `cexKernel` on `ℕ∞ ⊕ ℕ` has a Harris part `aₙ = inl n` from which it jumps with law `ν = Σₙ 2^{−(n+1)} δ_{aₙ}`, the limit point `x* = inl ⊤`, from which it jumps with law `½ν + ½δ_{e₀}`, and an escape path `eₖ = inr k`, along which it moves on with probability `βₖ = (k + 1)(k + 3)/(k + 2)²` and otherwise jumps with law `ν`. It is a `ν`-irreducible T-chain with `ν` maximal (`cex_isMaximalIrreducibility`, `cex_isTChain`); `x*` is topologically recurrent (`cex_isTopRecurrent`), but from `x*` the chain stays on the escape path forever with probability at least `¼` (`cex_escape`), so `x*` is not topologically Harris recurrent (`cex_not_isTopHarrisRecurrent`) and lies in no maximal Harris set. So `x*` lies in `N` for every decomposition, while being topologically recurrent (`theorem_9_3_6_counterexample`). What the proof shows, that every `x* ∈ N` has an open neighbourhood `O` with `O ∩ N` uniformly transient, and that `x*` is topologically transient when `T(x*, H) = 0` (in particular when the chain is transient, `H = ∅`), is proved (`theorem_9_3_6`). The same chain refutes the remark on p. 212 that for ψ-irreducible T-chains Theorem 9.3.6 excludes topologically recurrent states that are not topologically Harris recurrent. The statement's "`H` is non-empty or a maximal Harris set" is read as "empty or", as in Theorem 9.2.2. |
| Lemma 9.3.2, p. 209 | If `T(x*, B) > 0` for a continuous component `T` of `K_a`, some neighbourhood `O` of `x*` has `O ⇝ᵃ B`, "and hence from Lemma 5.5.1, `O ⇝ B`". | Lemma 5.5.1 needs `a(0) = 0`. ## Conventions

* Transition laws are Mathlib's kernels, and `Pⁿ` is the kernel power. `P_x` is the carried
  `pathKernel P x` on `ℕ → X`, `P_μ` is `pathMeasure P μ`, `θⁿ` is `shift n`, and `𝓕ₙ^Φ` is
  `natFilt n`, a Mathlib `MeasureTheory.Filtration`. `{Φ ∈ A i.o.}` is `ioSet A`, `Q(x, A)` is
  `harrisQ P A x`, `L(x, A)` is `hitProb P A x`, `P_x(σ_A < ∞)` is `hitProbZero P A x`, and
  `P_x(τ_A ≥ n)` is `tailProb P A n x`. `U` is `potKernel P`, `U_A` is `tabooPotKernel P hA`,
  `η_A` is `occTime A`, `τ_A` is `firstReturn A`, `σ_A` is `firstHit A`, `τ_A(k)` is
  `returnTimeK A k`, and `A⁰ = {L(·, A) = 0}` is `noHitSet P A`. `K_a` is `sampledKernel P a`,
  `D ⇝ A` is `UnifAccessible P D A`, and `B ⇝ᵃ A` is `SampledAccessible P a B A`. All of these
  except `ioSet` and `harrisQ` are carried.
* `U`, `L`, `Q` and occupation times take values in `[0, ∞]` (`ℝ≥0∞`), so "`U(x, A) < ∞`" is
  `U(x, A) ≠ ∞`.
* "`Φ` is ψ-irreducible" is `IsMaximalIrreducibility P ψ` (carried): `ψ ≠ 0` is a maximal
  irreducibility measure. `IsHarris P ψ` includes it, and `A ∈ B⁺(X)` is
  `MeasurableSet A ∧ 0 < ψ A`. A maximal Harris set is `IsMaximalHarrisSet P ψ H`, which includes
  that `H` is measurable and non-empty. Recurrent and transient chains and sets, and petite and
  absorbing sets, are the carried `IsRecurrentChain`, `IsTransientChain`, `IsRecurrentSet`,
  `IsTransientSet`, `IsUniformlyTransient`, `IsPetite` and `IsAbsorbing`.
* On a topological space, "neighbourhood" means open neighbourhood in `IsTopRecurrent`,
  `IsTopTransient` and `IsTopHarrisRecurrent`; by monotonicity of `U` and `Q` this is equivalent to
  the book's definitions. `{Φ → ∞}` is `tendsToInfty`. A coercive function is `V : X → ℝ≥0` with
  every `closure {V ≤ r}` compact (`IsCoercive`). Weak and strong Feller chains, T-chains and
  continuous components are the carried `IsWeakFeller`, `IsStrongFeller`, `IsTChain` and
  `IsContinuousComponent`.
* The condition (V1) is `∀ x ∉ C, drift P (fun y => (V y : ℝ≥0∞)) x ≤ 0` with `V : X → ℝ≥0`
  measurable (`drift`, carried); in `SupermartingaleDrift` it is written
  `∫ P(x, dy) V(y) ≤ V(x)` for `x ∉ C`. "Unbounded off petite sets" is
  `∀ n : ℕ, IsPetite P {x | V x ≤ n}`.
* The random walk on `ℤ` or `ℝ` is `randomWalkKernel Γ`, the walk on `ℝ₊` is
  `halfLineWalkKernel Γ` on `ℝ≥0`, the scalar linear model is `ar1Kernel Γ α`, and the SETAR model
  is `setarKernel Γ φ θ r` with regimes indexed by `Fin (m + 1)`: the book's regime `1` is `0` and
  regime `M` is `Fin.last m` (all carried). A random recursion is realised on the noise path space
  `(ℕ → W, Measure.infinitePi)` through `orbit` (carried) and `pathKernel_recursion`.
* Chains on `ℝ₊` in §9.5.3 are kernels on `ℝ≥0`; `m(x)` is `meanDrift P x`, `v(x)` is
  `incrementVar P x`, and `W_x = Φ₁ − x` under `P(x, ·)`. The `c/x` chain is `cxKernel c` on `ℕ`.
* On a countable space, recurrence and Harris recurrence are taken for counting measure
  (Mathlib's `Measure.count`).
* The carried modules are copied from `TransienceRecurrence` without change apart from the
  namespace. The corrections they contain belong to Chapters 1–6 and 8, are recorded in their
  module docstrings and in those projects, and are not repeated here.
