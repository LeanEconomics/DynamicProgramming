# Corrections and conventions

This file lists the places where a Lean statement differs from the book: a claim corrected, a
hypothesis made explicit, a proof replaced, or a convention chosen. Seven of the claims below are
false as printed. Six are refuted in the Chapter 11 modules, and the seventh by a counterexample
carried from Chapter 10; for each of them a corrected statement is proved. For the other entries
the table says why no counterexample is given. The last section lists what is not formalised.

## Conventions

* Transition laws are Mathlib's kernels, and `Pⁿ` is the kernel power. Measures, kernels and
  test functions of (V2) take values in `[0, ∞]` (`ℝ≥0∞`), so "`b < ∞`" is `b ≠ ∞`. (V2) is
  `DriftV2 P V b C`, `∫ P(x, dy) V(y) + 1 ≤ V(x) + b 1_C(x)` for every `x`, which avoids
  subtracting infinite values; `driftV2_iff_drift` relates it to the extended-valued drift `drift`
  (carried). The test functions of §11.5 are real valued, and (11.43) uses the extended norm `‖·‖ₑ`.
* `τ_A ≥ 1` and `σ_A ≥ 0` are `firstReturn A` and `firstHit A` (carried), with values in
  `ℕ ∪ {∞}` (`WithTop ℕ`); `E_x[τ_A]` is `meanReturnTime P A x` (carried) and `E_x[σ_A]` is
  `meanHitTime P A x`, both in `[0, ∞]` with `τ_A = ∞` counted as `∞`. `Σ_{k<τ} F_k` is
  `sumBefore τ F`. The path measures `P_x` and `P_μ` are `pathKernel P x` and `pathMeasure P μ`,
  the filtration `F_k^Φ` of (11.20) is `natFilt` on the path space `ℕ → X`, and `θ` is `shift`
  (all carried).
* `C` is regular is `IsRegularSet P ψ C`, which includes measurability; the chain is regular is
  `IsRegularChain P ψ`; "`Φ` restricted to `S` is regular", for an absorbing `S`, is
  `IsRegularOn P ψ S` (a countable union of regular sets covers `S`), since hitting times from `S`
  do not depend on the restriction. A regular measure is `IsRegularMeasure P ψ μ`.
* `G_A(x, f)` is `gKernelFn P A f x`, `U_A(x, f)` is `tabooPotFn P A f x`, and `V_A` is `regV P A`.
  `_APⁿ(x, ·)` is `tabooProb P A n x` and `U_A(x, ·)` is `tabooPotKernel P hA x` (carried).
* "`Φ` is ψ-irreducible" is `IsMaximalIrreducibility P ψ` (carried); Lemma 11.3.6 uses
  `IsPhiIrreducible P φ`. `A ∈ B⁺(X)` is `MeasurableSet A ∧ 0 < ψ A`. Full and absorbing sets are
  `IsFull ψ S` and `IsAbsorbing P S` (carried). A positive chain is `IsPositive P ψ`, a
  non-positive (null) chain `IsNull P ψ`, a positive Harris chain `IsPositiveHarris P ψ`.
* The lazy chain `½ (I + P)` is `lazyKernel P`, the sampled kernel with weights `lazyWeights`; the
  `m`-skeleton is `P ^ m`. The split chain of a minorization `M : Minorization P` is
  `M.splitKernel` (carried).
* The semi-dynamical system is a map `F : X → X` with iterates `F^[n]`, not a kernel; `σ_O` is
  `dsHit F O`.
* Random walk on a half line is `halfLineWalkKernel Γ` on `ℝ≥0`, the simple linear model
  `ar1Kernel Γ α` on `ℝ`, and the forward recurrence time chain `fwdRecKernel p` on `ℕ` with
  renewal state `0` (all carried). The SETAR model is `setarKernel Γ φ θ r` (carried) with regimes
  indexed by `Fin (m + 1)` and thresholds `r : Fin m → ℝ`; the book's `θ(1)`, `φ(1)` are `θ 0`,
  `φ 0`, and `θ(M)`, `φ(M)` are `θ (Fin.last m)`, `φ (Fin.last m)`. Lebesgue measure is Mathlib's
  `volume`.
* The GI/G/1 queue with re-entry is `gig1rKernel G H r` on `ℕ × ℝ≥0`, with `[0] = (0, 0)`; the
  book's `1/λ` and `1/μ` are `E T = ∫ t G(dt)` and `m = E S = ∫ s H(ds)`, and the re-entry decision
  has law `exitLaw r` on `Bool` (`true`: the customer leaves). The expected work `V(x) = E_x[W₀]`
  is `qworkV m r x`, given by its explicit formula.
* The carried modules are copied from `InvariantMeasures` and `PseudoAtoms` without change apart
  from the namespace and the module docstrings noted in the [README](../README.md). The corrections
  they contain belong to Chapters 1–6 and 8–10, are recorded in their module docstrings and in
  those projects, and are not repeated here.

## Not formalised

* **The queue on one probability space** (§11.4.2, pp. 274–276): the identification of the
  random recursion `gig1rKernel` with a queue built from global sequences of interarrival times,
  service times and re-entry decisions; the book asserts the Markov property. With it go the
  random variables `W_n`, the identities `V(x) = E[W_n | Φ_n = x]` and `PⁿV(x) = E_x[W_n]`, and the
  pathwise identity (11.35) over `k` intervals; the expected work is defined by its formula
  (`qworkV`) and (11.35) is proved in expectation for one interval.
* **The monotonicity remark** `P_x(Φ_m = [0]) ≤ P_y(Φ_m = [0])` for `x ≥ y` (p. 275), which the
  potential argument for Proposition 11.4.3 does not need.
* **§11.6**, the commentary (pp. 285–287).
* **Informal remarks.** The motivation through stationarity (p. 256); the deterministic system as
  a Feller Markov chain with `Pf = f ∘ F` (p. 261) and the analogy between ultimate boundedness and
  positivity (pp. 261, 263); "almost-regular" sets (p. 258); the remarks on weak irreducibility and
