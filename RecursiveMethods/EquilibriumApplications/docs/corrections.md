# Corrections and conventions

Places where a Lean statement differs from the book or the solutions manual (Irigoyen,
Rossi-Hansberg and Wright): a claim corrected, a hypothesis made explicit, a gap in a proof closed,
or a convention chosen. Every false claim below is refuted in Lean, by a counterexample or a proof
of inconsistency. The last section lists what is not proved. The manual has no solutions to
Exercises 16.2, 16.3e, 16.4, 16.5 and 16.7.

## Claims corrected

| §16.1, p. 476 | `f(k) = F(k, 1) + (1 − δ)k` with `F ≥ 0`, `δ ∈ (0, 1]`, is bounded. | Inconsistent unless `δ = 1`: `f(k) ≥ (1 − δ)k` (`gc_bounded_forces_full_depreciation`); for `δ = 1/2` no `F ≥ 0` makes `f` bounded (`gc_bounded_counterexample`). ## Conventions

* Time starts at `t = 0` throughout. In §§16.5 and 16.6 the book indexes from `t = 1`, so its
  period `t` is period `t − 1` here, and its `2⁻ᵗ` in §16.6 is `pecW t = 2^{−(t+1)}`.
* `ℓ∞` is `BddSeq ℝ` (Chapter 15), with sequences in a normed space `E` in §16.5
  (`BddSeq E`) and in `ℝˡ` (`Fin l → ℝ`, with Lean's max norm, equivalent to the book's
  Euclidean norm) in §16.7. `S_θ` is identified with `ℓ∞` by the isometry `sgOfBdd θ`, and
  subsets of `S_θ` are computed in `ℓ∞` coordinates (`sgTechθ`, `sgXa`).
* §16.1: the functional equations (1) and (2) are posed on `K × [0, k_max]` and `K × ℝ₊` with
  `K = [a, k_max]`, `a > 0`, and their operators are written as maxima over `z′ = s f(z)` and
  `a′ = s a/q(k)`, `s ∈ [0, 1]` (`gcFirmT`, `gcHhT`).
* §16.2: the shock histories `(Z^t, 𝒵^t, μ^t)` are probability spaces with maps `proj t` (drop the
  last shock) and `ζ t` (the current shock); the commodity space is `LinftyTimeState μ` of
  Chapter 15. `U` is continuous and bounded on all of `ℝ`; only its values on `ℝ₊` matter. The
  continuity of `Φ` (and of the §16.7 technology) is nonempty values with upper and lower
  hemicontinuity in sequential form (`MsTech`, `ManyConsumersTech`). In the securities problem
  the state is `s = (x, z)`, the next state is `nxt s z′` and the transition is `π s z′`
  (`MsSecData`) or `Q(s, ·)` (`MsFellerData`); a security pays the return `R(s, z′)`, which is
  `f(z′)` for a one-period security and `f(s′) + q(s′)` for the asset of Exercise 16.2g.
* The cross-reference "Section 13.1" on p. 481 is read as §13.3, the stochastic growth model.
* §16.3: CRRA utility with `σ = 1` is `log` (`sgCRRA`, `sgCRRA_one`); the consumption set of
  Exercises 16.3d–e is `X = {x ∈ S_θ⁺ : x_t ≥ aθᵗ}` (`sgXa`).
* §16.4: the shock histories are one probability space `(Ω, μ)` with a filtration `ℱ`, and
  `L∞(Z^t, 𝒵^t, μ^t)` is the bounded `ℱ_t`-measurable functions. Plans are functions and the
  constraints of (1) hold at every point; equivalence classes enter only in `IndustryLinfty`,
  where two goods per period are coordinates `2t` and `2t + 1` of `LinftyTimeState`. `c` and `D`
  are continuous on all of `ℝ` (resp. `ℝ²`); the `C¹` hypothesis on `c` is not needed. The
  normed space `IndustrySpace` has the norm `sup_{t, ω} max(\|q_t(ω)\|, \|I_t(ω)\|)`, equivalent
  to the book's `sup_t (‖q_t‖_∞ + ‖I_t‖_∞)`.
* §16.7: consumers are a finite type `ι`; `mcSimplex ι` is `Δᴵ`. Functions `f : ℝᵖ → ℝᴵ → ℝ`
  are used only on `ℝᵖ₊ × Δᴵ`. (W1)–(W3) and continuity are `IsAggregator` of Exercise 15.10;
  (W4)–(W5) are fields of `ManyConsumersAggregator`. "Attains the supremum in (2)" is read as
  maximising `Σᵢ θᵢ uᵢ(cᵢ)` over the allocations feasible from `κ`.
* The manual's `B(y, 5)`, `5/4` and `5/2` in Exercise 16.6(d) are a scanning artefact for
  `B(y, ε)`, `ε/4` and `ε/2`; its proof is otherwise correct (`ex_16_6d`).
* The concrete economies are not in the book: `gcRatio` (§16.1); the log-utility economy
  `gcLogG`, `gcLogC`, `gcLogDU`, `gcLogDF` (Exercise 16.1e); `msPhiEps`, `msPhiZero`
  (Exercise 16.2a); the economies in `ex_16_2c_domain_counterexample`,
  `ex_16_2d_counterexample` and `ex_16_2h_bubble_counterexample`; `indLinftyPlan` (§16.4);
  `mcExPhi`, `mcBox`, `mcExW`, `mcExW2`, `mcExFd`, `mcExFe`, `mcExG` (§16.7).

## Not proved

* **The plan `γ*` of §10.3.** That `γ*` maximises `u` on `C ∩ Y` (Exercise 10.3b) is a hypothesis
  of Exercise 16.2b (`ex_16_2b`); the §10.3 dynamic program is not reproduced here.
* **Continuity of `w` in the state.** For a general measurable shock space and a merely
  measurable return, Exercise 16.2c is proved with `w` measurable in the state and continuous in
  `A` (`ex_16_2c_general`); `w` need not be continuous in the state, so "bounded continuous" in
  the book is replaced by this class. Uniqueness and (2)–(3) are for measurable `w` bounded on
  `D`, since for a non-measurable `w` the integral in (1) is meaningless.
* **Exercise 16.1d at a corner.** `ex_16_1d` and `ex_16_1d_unique` assume an interior allocation.
  The transcription's corner case (relative prices pinned down only to an interval when, for
  example, `x⁰₀ = 0`) has no Lean counterexample.
* **Exercise 16.1e–g in general.** With `g` the policy of (3), the exercise is proved under the
  §6.1 hypotheses (Inada conditions, continuous differentiability, `U` bounded) and `k̂ ≤ k̄`
  (`ex_16_1efg`, `ex_16_1efg_exists`); for `k̂ > k̄` the state space `[0, k̂]` would be needed.
  Otherwise the properties of `g` in `GrowthCertaintyPlanner` are hypotheses.
* **The optimum in §16.3.** Exercise 16.3e(ii) assumes a Pareto optimum whose lower bound
  `x_t ≥ aθᵗ` does not bind (`ex_16_3e_ii`, `ex_16_3e_ii_direct`). Neither the existence of an
  optimum nor the non-binding bound is proved.
* **Uniqueness of the industry equilibrium.** The claim of p. 490 that, with §10.4, there is at
  most one industry equilibrium rests on the uniqueness of the surplus maximum of §10.4, which is
  not in this project; existence of a surplus maximum is likewise not proved.
* **The supporting price of Exercise 16.4(e).** Theorem 15.4 gives some supporting `φ`
  (`ex_16_4e_c`); that it is the (I2) price functional, or has an inner-product form, is not
  proved. Exercise 16.4c itself is proved directly (`ex_16_4c`).
* **The cheaper point in §16.7.** `mc_competitiveEq_of_pos` assumes `φ(cᵢ) > 0` for every
  consumer; it can fail for a consumer whose bundle has zero value.
* **The utility functions `u_W` in §16.7.** Their existence and uniqueness (Exercise 5.11d) is a
  hypothesis throughout (`IsAggregatorFixedPoint`).
* **The capital bound in §16.7.** The corrected Exercise 16.7c as stated, the continuity of `v`
  and Exercise 16.7g for `f = v` rest on (T8) (`ex_16_7c_of_bound`, `mcSupport_inF`,
  `ex_16_7g_support`); without it, closedness of `U(κ)` must be assumed, and it can fail
  (`ex_16_7c_counterexample`).
* **The chapter introduction, the remarks** (p. 481 on interpretation, pp. 484–485 on stock
  prices), the drawing asked for in Exercise 16.7f, and §16.8 (bibliographic notes) are not
  formalised.
