# SLP Chapter 7: Measure Theory and Integration, in Lean

This is the `RecursiveMethods/MeasureIntegration` proof project in
[LeanEconomics/DynamicProgramming](https://github.com/LeanEconomics/DynamicProgramming). It covers
Chapter 7 of Stokey and Lucas with Prescott, *Recursive Methods in Economic Dynamics* (1989). Run
the commands below from this directory. [Return to the library index](../../README.md).

Chapter 7 sets out the measure theory used for stochastic dynamic programming. The book's
definitions are stated as given: σ-algebras, measures, measures on algebras, completions,
measurable functions, the integral as a supremum over simple functions, integrable functions,
monotone classes and conditional expectations. Each is identified with its Mathlib counterpart,
whose API carries the later results. Every numbered result (Theorems 7.1–7.16 with 7.2′, Lemmas
7.7, 7.9, 7.12 and 7.15) and every exercise (7.1–7.33) is formalised. Theorem 7.6, which the
book cites, is proved. The corrections are recorded in [corrections](docs/corrections.md). The
library contains **226 theorems**.

* **Measurable spaces and measures** (§7.1–7.2).
  * σ-algebras, Borel sets, Exercises 7.1–7.3. The Borel sets of a metric space are generated by
    the balls only when it is separable (`discrete_balls_ne_top`).
  * Measures, Theorem 7.1 by the book's proof, and Exercises 7.4–7.5.
  * Algebras and the extension theorems 7.2, 7.2′ and 7.3. The algebra of half-open intervals
    (Exercise 7.6), length (Exercise 7.7) and the uniqueness of Lebesgue measure
    (`length_unique`). Completions (Exercise 7.8).
* **Measurable functions** (§7.3).
  * Exercises 7.9–7.15, Theorem 7.4 by the book's proof, and Theorem 7.5 by the book's dyadic
    construction.
  * The Baire functions are exactly the Borel functions (Exercise 7.13b, `ex_7_13b`).
  * The Measurable Selection Theorem 7.6, proved by lexicographic minimization (`theorem_7_6`).
* **Integration** (§7.4).
  * The integral of simple functions and of `M⁺`, identified with Mathlib's `lintegral`.
  * Lemma 7.7, the Monotone Convergence Theorem 7.8 and Fatou's Lemma 7.9, all by the book's
    proofs, with Exercises 7.16–7.24.
  * Integrable functions, Exercises 7.25–7.26, and the Dominated Convergence Theorem 7.10.
  * The Radon–Nikodym Theorem 7.11: the density need not be integrable when `λ` is infinite
    (`theorem_7_11_not_integrable`). Lemma 7.12 by the book's proof.
* **Product spaces** (§7.5).
  * Finite unions of rectangles (Exercise 7.27), `𝓑^{k+l} = 𝓑^k × 𝓑^l` (Exercise 7.28),
    Theorem 7.13 with countable additivity proved, finite products (Exercise 7.29), and sections
    (Theorem 7.14).
  * `ℒ² ≠ ℒ¹ × ℒ¹` (Exercise 7.30), through a Vitali set constructed here
    (`vitaliSet_not_nullMeasurable`).
* **The Monotone Class Lemma** (§7.6): the four facts, and Lemma 7.15 by the book's proof.
* **Conditional expectation** (§7.7).
  * Theorem 7.16 by the book's route through the Radon–Nikodym Theorem, uniqueness, and the law
    of iterated expectations.
  * Countable partitions (Exercise 7.32). On the unit square, conditioning on the first
    coordinate averages over the second with weights `p(x, ·)` (Exercise 7.33).

All names are in the namespace `StokeyLucasPrescott.MeasureIntegration`. See the
[source map](docs/source-map.md) and the [corrections](docs/corrections.md).

The module `Correspondences` is carried over from the Chapter 3 project
(`RecursiveMethods/MathematicalPreliminaries`), so that the project is self-contained.

## Build and verify

```sh
lake exe cache get
python scripts/verify.py
```

The verifier builds the library and freshly recompiles every contributed proof. It also audits
each named declaration's axioms: only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` are allowed.
Warnings fail the check.
